HANKO — TWO-MINUTE DEMO

Bracketed lines are cues, not spoken words.
Target: 35 seconds opening, 20 seconds setup, 45 seconds call,
20 seconds close. A live call can run longer; rehearse the timing.


[OPENING]

In a Japanese survey, three out of four IT staff said
parts of their systems could stop working if a key veteran left.

So imagine Tanaka-san.
He maintained this billing system for years.
He's now retired, but stays on as a technical adviser.

AI takes on the code changes.
But the current team doesn't have all the history behind them.

Hango brings Tanaka in when company policy flags
a critical change in his area,
and records his decision for the team.


[SHOW THE REQUEST, IMPACT AND EDIT]

Here's our fictional COBOL billing system.
We're changing a tax rate from eight to ten percent
for over twelve hundred customers.

Devin prepared this edit ahead of the demo.
Codex independently compiled and tested it.
Our policy names Tanaka as the approver for tax changes.

Hango calls him through ElevenLabs. I'll play Tanaka.


[START THE CALL — STOP NARRATING AND LET THE AGENT SPEAK]

[When asked if now is a good time]

Yes, go ahead.

[After the agent explains the change and asks for a decision]

I approve, with one condition.
Check the rounding on invoices under 100 yen.
Small invoices always caused trouble in 2009.

[Wait for the readback AND final approval confirmation question]

Yes, I confirm approval with that condition.

[Let the call end. Point to the saved decision.]


[CLOSE]

Tanaka didn't have to write the code or return to daily maintenance.
He gave a decision, a condition, and the reason behind it.

Hango keeps those with the exact change.
The condition becomes a task, and release stays held.

Experienced developers can step back.
Their judgment stays available, and the next team gets the context.
